Executive Summary

The dataset contains 3,000 customer records with no missing values detected in the supplied Banking.csv. The analysis shows meaningful differences in financial balances across income bands and risk-weighting groups.

The strongest measured relationship is between Bank Deposits and Checking Accounts (r = 0.844). Bank Deposits also have a positive relationship with Saving Accounts (r = 0.755). Income has a moderate positive relationship with Bank Loans (r = 0.330).

These are associations, not causal relationships. Business recommendations should therefore be validated using profitability, customer response, credit policy and operational constraints.

Key Findings

1Deposits & checking are closely related

Bank Deposits and Checking Accounts have a correlation of 0.844, indicating a strong positive linear association in this dataset.

Business interpretation: customers with substantial deposit balances can be examined for relationship-deepening opportunities around transaction and savings products.

2Higher income aligns with larger balances

Average Bank Deposits increase from $486,223 in the Low income band to $929,822 in the High income band.

Average loans and business lending also increase across the same income bands.

3Risk-weighting shows different exposure levels

The dataset contains five risk-weighting levels. Risk 2 is the largest group with 1,222 customers. Average loan and business-lending balances differ across the risk groups.

This can support portfolio monitoring and segmentation, subject to the bank's actual risk definitions and policies.

4Customer mix is concentrated in High income

The High income band contains 456 customers (15.2%), making it the largest of the three income segments defined for this analysis.

The Fee Structure distribution is also uneven, with High as the largest category (1,476 customers).

Recommendations

1. Deposit & savings cross-sell

Use deposit and checking balances to identify customers for savings and relationship-deepening campaigns.

  • Target customers with relatively high deposits and lower savings balances.
  • Track conversion, incremental deposits and products per customer.

2. Income-based customer journeys

Create differentiated product journeys because financial balances vary across Low, Mid and High income bands.

  • Low: savings and foundational banking products.
  • Mid: lending and savings combinations.
  • High: evaluate premium, investment and wealth-oriented services where appropriate.

3. Combine risk with exposure

Use Risk Weighting together with loan and business-lending balances for portfolio monitoring and credit-review prioritization.

  • Investigate high-exposure customers within higher-risk groups.
  • Do not use correlation alone as a credit decision rule.

4. Deepen business relationships

Business Lending has a positive association with Bank Loans (r = 0.417) and Bank Deposits (r = 0.441).

  • Identify customers combining business lending with meaningful deposit balances.
  • Evaluate cash-management and working-capital opportunities.
